Subject 05 · Biotechnology Curriculum

Nanotechnology.

Nanotechnology is the study and application of materials and systems at the nanoscale, where size-dependent properties can enable new approaches to sensing, imaging, biomaterials and other biotechnology applications.

Nanoscale MaterialsNanoparticlesNano–Bio InterfacesBiotechnology
01 · Scientific Overview

What is Nanotechnology?

Nanotechnology concerns the design, characterization and application of materials and structures with nanoscale dimensions. At these dimensions, changes in surface area, interfaces and other size-dependent properties can influence how materials interact with their environment.

For biotechnology students, nanotechnology provides a framework for studying nano–bio interfaces, nanoscale sensing, biomaterials and other emerging approaches in biological research.

02 · Learning Objectives

What should students understand?

Explain the nanoscale and why material properties can change with size and structure.

Identify major classes and forms of nanomaterials used in research.

Describe how nanoparticles can interact with biomolecules and biological interfaces.

Understand basic principles of nano-enabled sensing and analytical detection.

Explain major biotechnology application areas of nanotechnology.

Recognize the importance of characterization, safety and evidence when evaluating nanomaterials.

03 · Core Concepts

The scientific language of nanotechnology

01

Nanoscale

The nanoscale describes dimensions commonly associated with approximately 1–100 nanometres, where interfaces and size-dependent behavior can become especially important.

02

Nanomaterials

Nanomaterials can include nanoparticles, nanostructured surfaces, nanocomposites and other engineered nanoscale architectures.

03

Nano–Bio Interfaces

A nano–bio interface describes interactions between nanoscale materials and biological molecules, membranes, cells or tissues.

04

Characterization

Nanomaterials require appropriate structural, chemical, physical and surface characterization before biological interpretation.

04 · Nanoparticles & Nano–Bio Interface

How do nanomaterials interact with biological systems?

When a nanomaterial comes into contact with a biological environment, proteins and other biomolecules can associate with its surface. The resulting interface can influence cellular interactions, transport, recognition and biological responses.

Scientific perspective: nano–bio interactions depend on factors such as particle size, surface chemistry, composition, morphology, biological environment and exposure conditions.

06 · Biotechnology Connection

Why does Nanotechnology matter in Biotechnology?

From material design to biological function

Biotechnology can use nanoscale approaches to investigate biological interactions, improve analytical sensitivity, develop engineered surfaces, study biomaterials and create new experimental platforms. The scientific value of a nanotechnology application depends on reproducible characterization, biological evidence and appropriate safety evaluation.

07 · Applications in Biotechnology

Where does this knowledge lead?

Biosensing

Nanostructured materials can be investigated as components of sensing platforms designed to improve signal generation or detection.

Diagnostics

Nano-enabled approaches can support experimental diagnostic systems for detecting biological molecules and other analytical targets.

Imaging

Some nanomaterials have been investigated as imaging-related probes or contrast-enhancing platforms in biological research.

Drug Delivery Research

Nanostructured delivery systems are studied for their potential to influence the transport and release of bioactive molecules.

Biomaterials

Nanostructured surfaces and composites can be investigated for interactions with cells, tissues and biological environments.

Environmental Biotechnology

Nano-enabled materials and sensors are also investigated for detection, monitoring and treatment-related environmental applications.

08 · Scientific Perspective

At the interface of materials and life

Nanotechnology provides a scientific bridge between materials science, chemistry and biology. Its importance in biotechnology arises from the ability to engineer and characterize matter at dimensions where surface interactions and biological interfaces become especially significant.

For biotechnology students, the most important perspective is not simply that materials are “nano,” but that their structure, surface chemistry, characterization and interaction with biological systems determine their behavior and potential application.
