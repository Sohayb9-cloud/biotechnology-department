Analytical Chemistry.

Analytical chemistry is the science of measurement, separation and identification used to obtain accurate and reliable information about chemical and biological samples.

Measurement Separation Identification Quantitative Analysis
01 · Scientific Overview

What is Analytical Chemistry?

Analytical chemistry focuses on obtaining chemical information from samples. It includes qualitative analysis, which addresses what substances are present, and quantitative analysis, which determines how much of a substance is present.

Modern analytical workflows often combine sampling, sample preparation, separation, detection, calibration and data interpretation. In biotechnology, these approaches are used to investigate biological materials, metabolites, natural products, proteins, nucleic acids and other measurable components.

02 · Learning Objectives

What should students understand?

Distinguish qualitative and quantitative analytical approaches.

Understand the role of sampling and sample preparation in reliable analysis.

Explain the principles of titration and volumetric analysis.

Explain how chromatographic methods separate components of complex mixtures.

Understand the analytical role of spectroscopy and calibration.

Interpret analytical signals and connect measurements with quantitative results.

04 · Titration & Volumetric Analysis

Quantitative analysis by titration

Titration is a quantitative analytical technique used to determine the concentration of an analyte by reacting it with a solution of known concentration, called the titrant. The volume required to reach a defined endpoint is used, together with reaction stoichiometry, to calculate the analyte concentration.

Core idea: the analytical result depends on accurate volume measurement, appropriate reaction stoichiometry and a clearly defined endpoint or equivalence point.

Common titration approaches

Important categories include acid–base titration, redox titration, precipitation titration and complexometric titration. The appropriate method depends on the analyte and the chemical reaction used for measurement.

05 · Separation & Chromatography

Separating complex mixtures before identification

Chromatography separates components of a mixture according to differences in their interactions with a stationary phase and a mobile phase. Separation can simplify complex biological and chemical samples and provide information such as retention time and peak area.

Biotechnology relevance: chromatography is relevant to the analysis of metabolites, proteins, natural products and other components of complex biological samples.
06 · Spectroscopy & Quantitative Analysis

From molecular interaction to analytical data

Spectroscopic methods obtain analytical information from interactions between electromagnetic radiation and matter. In quantitative analysis, measured signals can be related to concentration through suitable calibration procedures.

Quantitative principle: a measured analytical response can be compared with standards or a calibration model to estimate the concentration of an unknown sample.
07 · Biotechnology Connection

Why does Analytical Chemistry matter in Biotechnology?

From sample to reliable scientific evidence

Biotechnology depends on measurements that are accurate, reproducible and interpretable. Analytical chemistry provides a methodological bridge between a biological sample and the quantitative or qualitative information needed to understand it.

08 · Applications in Biotechnology

Where does this knowledge lead?

Biological Sample Analysis

Analytical methods can be used to characterize components of biological, food, environmental and research samples.

Natural Products

Separation and detection approaches support the characterization of plant-derived compounds and complex natural-product mixtures.

Bioprocess Monitoring

Analytical measurements can help monitor substrates, products and other chemical changes during biological processes.

Quality & Validation

Reliable analytical procedures support quality assessment, method validation and scientific confidence in measured results.

09 · Scientific Perspective

Measurement turns observation into evidence

Analytical chemistry is not simply a collection of laboratory techniques. It provides a framework for turning a sample into reliable chemical information through controlled measurement, appropriate separation, validated procedures and careful interpretation of data.

For biotechnology students, this perspective is essential because biological research increasingly depends on quantitative evidence — from metabolite profiling and natural-product analysis to quality assessment and experimental validation.
