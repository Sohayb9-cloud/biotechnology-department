01 · Scientific Overview

What is Organic Chemistry?

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry concerned primarily with the structure, properties, reactions and synthesis of carbon-containing compounds. Carbon is particularly versatile because it can form stable covalent bonds with itself and with elements such as hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen, producing an enormous diversity of molecular structures.

This makes organic chemistry especially important in the life sciences. Proteins, carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids all contain extensive carbon frameworks, while functional groups help determine the chemical behavior and biological properties of many molecules.

02 · Learning Objectives

What should students understand?

Explain how carbon bonding contributes to molecular diversity and structural complexity.

Recognize major organic functional groups and relate them to chemical properties.

Understand the relationship between molecular structure and chemical reactivity.

Connect fundamental organic chemistry with biological molecules and biochemical processes.

03 · Core Concepts

The molecular language of organic chemistry

01

Carbon Skeletons

Carbon atoms can form chains, branches and rings. These frameworks provide the structural backbone of many organic molecules.

02

Functional Groups

Specific groups of atoms give organic molecules characteristic chemical properties and influence their reactions.

03

Isomerism

Molecules can share the same molecular formula while differing in connectivity or three-dimensional arrangement.

04

Reaction Mechanisms

Organic reactions can be understood through changes in bonds, electron distribution and reactive intermediates.

05

Polarity

Differences in electronegativity and molecular structure influence polarity, intermolecular interactions and solubility.

06

Stereochemistry

Three-dimensional molecular arrangement can strongly influence biological recognition and molecular function.

04 · Functional Groups

Small structural changes, major chemical consequences

Functional groups are characteristic groups of atoms within organic molecules. They influence polarity, acidity, basicity, hydrogen bonding and chemical reactivity.

Functional Group General Representation Biological Relevance
Hydroxyl –OH Common in alcohols and carbohydrates; contributes to polarity and hydrogen bonding.
Carbonyl C=O Found in aldehydes, ketones and many biologically important molecules.
Carboxyl –COOH Important in amino acids, fatty acids and many organic acids.
Amino –NH₂ A defining group in amino acids and many biologically active compounds.
Phosphate –PO₄ Central to nucleic acids and energy-transfer molecules such as ATP.
Sulfhydryl –SH Important in cysteine and in protein structure through disulfide formation.
05 · Biotechnology Connection

Why does Organic Chemistry matter in Biotechnology?

From carbon chemistry to biological function

Biotechnology investigates biological systems at multiple levels, and molecular structure is one of the foundations connecting chemistry with biology. Understanding organic chemistry helps students interpret the structures and reactions of biomolecules, metabolites and other biologically relevant compounds.

06 · Biotechnology Applications

Where does this knowledge lead?

Biochemistry

Organic chemistry provides the molecular framework for understanding carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, nucleic acids and metabolic reactions.

Natural Products

Many biologically active natural products are organic compounds whose structures and functional groups influence their chemical and biological properties.

Pharmaceutical Biotechnology

Molecular structure and reactivity are important for understanding drugs, bioactive compounds and interactions between molecules and biological targets.

Metabolic Science

Cellular metabolism involves networks of chemical transformations that follow the principles of molecular structure and organic reactivity.

07 · Scientific Perspective

Organic chemistry as a bridge

Organic chemistry is more than the memorization of molecular structures and reactions. It provides a framework for understanding how molecular architecture influences chemical behavior — and how chemical behavior ultimately contributes to biological function.

For biotechnology students, this perspective creates an important bridge between chemistry and disciplines such as biochemistry, molecular biology, pharmacology, plant biotechnology and natural-product research.
