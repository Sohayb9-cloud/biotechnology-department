Subject 04 · Biotechnology Curriculum

Biochemistry.

Biochemistry is the study of the chemical processes and molecular interactions that underlie living systems, including the structure, function and metabolism of proteins, carbohydrates, lipids and nucleic acids.

Biomolecules Enzymes ATP & Energy Metabolism
01 · Scientific Overview

What is Biochemistry?

Biochemistry connects chemistry with biology by examining the molecules, reactions and molecular interactions that make cellular life possible. It studies the structures and functions of biological macromolecules and the chemical transformations that occur within living systems.

For biotechnology students, biochemistry provides a molecular framework for understanding cellular metabolism, enzyme activity, biomolecular structure, energy transfer and many processes used in biotechnology research and development.

02 · Learning Objectives

What should students understand?

Identify the major classes of biomolecules and relate their structures to biological functions.

Explain the structural organization and functional roles of proteins and enzymes.

Describe major principles of carbohydrate, lipid and nucleic-acid biochemistry.

Explain how enzymes influence the rates of biochemical reactions.

Understand how ATP participates in biological energy transfer.

Connect metabolic pathways with cellular function and biotechnology applications.

03 · Core Concepts

The molecular language of life

01

Proteins

Proteins are polymers of amino acids whose three-dimensional structures enable diverse biological functions, including catalysis, transport, structural support and molecular recognition.

02

Carbohydrates

Carbohydrates participate in energy storage, metabolism, structural organization and molecular recognition.

03

Lipids

Lipids contribute to membrane structure, energy storage and signaling, with diverse chemical structures and functions.

04

Nucleic Acids

DNA and RNA store, transmit or participate in the expression and regulation of biological information.

04 · Proteins & Enzymes

Structure, Function and Molecular Catalysis

Protein function depends strongly on molecular structure. Enzymes are biological catalysts that can accelerate biochemical reactions by providing pathways with lower activation-energy barriers.

Key concept: enzyme catalysis changes reaction rate by lowering the activation-energy barrier; it does not mean the enzyme is consumed as a reactant.
05 · Biochemical Pathways & Metabolism

From Nutrients to Cellular Energy

Metabolism consists of interconnected chemical pathways that transform nutrients into energy, cellular building blocks and metabolic intermediates. Central pathways include glycolysis, the citric acid cycle and oxidative phosphorylation.

06 · Biotechnology Connection

Why does Biochemistry matter in Biotechnology?

From molecular chemistry to biological technology

Biotechnology frequently works at the molecular level. Understanding proteins, enzymes, nucleic acids, metabolites and energy-transfer processes helps students interpret cellular behavior and understand processes used in molecular biology, fermentation, diagnostics, bioprocessing and biomolecule production.

07 · Applications in Biotechnology

Where does this knowledge lead?

Enzyme Technology

Enzymes are studied and used as biological catalysts in research, industrial biocatalysis and biotechnology processes.

Fermentation & Bioprocessing

Biochemical principles help explain substrate utilization, metabolite formation, energy metabolism and product generation.

Protein Biotechnology

Biochemistry supports the study, production, purification and functional analysis of recombinant and native proteins.

Metabolic Biotechnology

Understanding metabolic pathways supports research into metabolic engineering, biomolecule production and cellular bioprocesses.

08 · Scientific Perspective

Biochemistry Connects Molecules to Life

Biochemistry provides the molecular framework needed to understand how biological structures perform their functions and how chemical reactions are coordinated within living cells.

For biotechnology students, this perspective connects molecular structure, catalytic activity, metabolism and cellular regulation with the development and analysis of biological products, processes and technologies.
