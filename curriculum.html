Medicinal Plants

Plant Biology · Phytochemistry · Natural Products

Medicinal plants contain naturally occurring bioactive compounds that may produce therapeutic or beneficial pharmacological effects. This subject explores medicinal plant diversity, specialized metabolites, phytochemistry, extraction, biological activity and plant biotechnology.

Phytochemicals Natural Products Plant Metabolism Tissue Culture
FULL SUBJECT

Medicinal Plants

Scientific Definition

Medicinal plants are plant species or plant-derived materials that contain biologically active constituents capable of producing therapeutic or other beneficial physiological effects. Their biological activity is associated with chemically diverse primary and specialized metabolites whose abundance and composition can vary among species, tissues, developmental stages and environmental conditions.

Scientific Overview

Medicinal Plants examines plant resources as sources of biologically active natural products. The course integrates plant biology with phytochemistry by considering botanical identification, plant organs and tissues, specialized metabolism, major classes of phytochemicals and their biosynthetic origins. It also introduces extraction and characterization of plant-derived compounds, evaluation of biological activity, and the use of plant tissue culture for controlled propagation, conservation and metabolite production.

Core Areas

Medicinal plant identification
Plant organs and tissues
Primary and specialized metabolism
Alkaloids and phenolic compounds
Flavonoids and terpenoids
Biosynthetic pathways
Extraction and characterization
Biological activity
Plant tissue culture

Biotechnology Relevance

The subject connects plant science and phytochemistry with natural-product biotechnology, plant tissue culture, conservation, controlled metabolite production and the investigation of biologically active compounds.

VISUAL LEARNING

Scientific Visuals

02 VISUALS

Learning Outcomes

  1. Define medicinal plants in a scientific context and explain their importance as sources of biologically active natural products.
  2. Identify major classes of plant specialized metabolites and relate them to plant tissues and biological functions.
  3. Explain the principles of extraction and characterization of plant-derived compounds.
  4. Describe the role of plant tissue culture in propagation, conservation and metabolite production.

Analytical Chemistry

Measurement · Separation · Quantitative Analysis

Analytical chemistry provides the principles required to identify, separate and quantitatively measure chemical substances in biological and non-biological samples.

Measurement Titration Chromatography Spectroscopy
FULL SUBJECT

Analytical Chemistry

Scientific Definition

Analytical chemistry is the branch of chemistry concerned with obtaining qualitative and quantitative information about the composition of matter. It develops methods for identifying chemical species, determining their amounts and evaluating the reliability of analytical measurements.

Scientific Overview

Analytical Chemistry focuses on the analytical process from sampling and sample preparation through separation, measurement and interpretation. The course introduces qualitative and quantitative analysis, chemical equilibrium, volumetric methods, calibration and instrumental techniques including chromatography and spectroscopy. Particular emphasis is placed on accuracy, precision, analytical signals and the quality of experimental data.

Core Areas

Qualitative analysis
Quantitative analysis
Chemical equilibrium
Volumetric analysis
Calibration
Sample preparation
Chromatographic separation
Spectroscopic analysis

Biotechnology Relevance

Analytical chemistry supports biotechnology through biomolecule characterization, metabolite analysis, quality control, biological sample measurement, pharmaceutical analysis and quantitative assessment of experimental results.

VISUAL LEARNING

Scientific Visuals

02 VISUALS

Learning Outcomes

  1. Distinguish between qualitative and quantitative analytical approaches.
  2. Explain how analytical methods generate measurable signals and chemical information.
  3. Describe the principles of titration, chromatography and spectroscopy.
  4. Evaluate the importance of accuracy, precision, calibration and data reliability in analysis.

Organic Chemistry

Carbon Chemistry · Functional Groups · Reactivity

Organic chemistry explains how carbon-containing molecules are structured, how their functional groups determine chemical behavior, and how organic reactions transform them.

Carbon Structures Functional Groups Isomerism Reaction Mechanisms
FULL SUBJECT

Organic Chemistry

Scientific Definition

Organic chemistry is the branch of chemistry that studies the structure, properties, reactions and synthesis of compounds containing carbon, with particular emphasis on molecular bonding, functional groups, stereochemistry and chemical reactivity.

Scientific Overview

Organic Chemistry examines carbon-based molecular structures and the relationship between molecular architecture, functional groups, physical properties and chemical reactivity. The course introduces hydrocarbons, structural and stereoisomerism, alcohols, ethers, carbonyl compounds, carboxylic acids and other biologically relevant organic compounds, together with the mechanisms of major organic reactions.

Core Areas

Functional groups
Structural isomerism
Stereoisomerism
Reaction mechanisms
Alcohols and ethers
Carbonyl compounds
Carboxylic acids

Biotechnology Relevance

Organic chemistry provides the chemical foundation required to understand biomolecules, metabolites, natural products, pharmaceutical compounds and the molecular transformations that occur in biological systems.

02 VISUALS

  4. Explain the role of organic chemistry in understanding biologically important compounds.

Biochemistry

Biomolecules · Enzymes · Metabolism

Biochemistry investigates the molecules and chemical reactions that constitute living systems and support cellular function.

FULL SUBJECT

Biochemistry

Scientific Definition

Biochemistry is the branch of science concerned with the chemical constituents of living organisms, their molecular structures, and the chemical reactions and pathways through which biological systems maintain organization, obtain energy and perform cellular functions.

Scientific Overview

Biochemistry examines the molecular composition of living systems and the chemical reactions that sustain cellular function. It focuses on proteins, amino acids, carbohydrates, lipids, nucleic acids and their relationships with biological function. The course also addresses enzyme catalysis, regulation, bioenergetics and the integration of biochemical pathways into coordinated metabolic networks.

Core Areas

Protein structure and function

Enzyme regulation
Carbohydrate metabolism
Lipid metabolism
Nucleotides and nucleic acids
ATP and bioenergetics
Integrated metabolic pathways

Biotechnology Relevance

Biochemistry provides a core scientific foundation for enzyme technology, fermentation, metabolic engineering, molecular biotechnology, diagnostics and biomedical applications.

Nanotechnology examines materials and structures at dimensions where size-dependent properties become important for biological and technological applications.

Nanotechnology

Scientific Definition

Nanotechnology is the study and controlled manipulation of matter at the nanoscale, where dimensions and surface characteristics can produce physical, chemical and biological properties that differ from those of the same material at larger scales.

Scientific Overview

The course introduces nanomaterials and nanoparticles, their size-dependent properties and surface behavior, together with their interaction with biological systems. It also considers preparation and characterization of nanomaterials and their applications in biosensing, molecular delivery, imaging and biomedical materials.

Cytogenetics

Chromosomes · Karyotype · Chromosomal Abnormalities

Cytogenetics is the branch of genetics that studies the structure, function, behavior and abnormalities of chromosomes at the cellular level.

FULL SUBJECT

Cytogenetics

Scientific Definition

Cytogenetics is a branch of genetics that studies the structure, organization, behavior and abnormalities of chromosomes, usually through their examination in dividing or non-dividing cells.

Scientific Overview

Cytogenetics focuses on chromosome number, morphology, structural organization and cellular behavior. The course introduces karyotyping and chromosome analysis and examines numerical abnormalities such as aneuploidy together with structural changes including deletions, duplications, inversions and translocations. It also considers the relationship between chromosomal abnormalities, genetic disease and cancer.

Core Areas

Chromosome organization

Structural abnormalities
Deletion and duplication
Inversion
Translocation

Biotechnology Relevance

Cytogenetics supports genetic diagnosis, chromosome analysis, cancer cytogenetics, prenatal investigation and research into structural and numerical variation of the genome.

Learning Outcomes

Biofertilizers

Beneficial Microorganisms · Nutrient Cycling · Sustainable Agriculture

Biofertilizers are biological preparations containing beneficial living microorganisms that improve nutrient availability, plant nutrition, soil biological activity and plant growth through processes such as biological nitrogen fixation and nutrient mobilization.

Nitrogen Fixation Phosphate Solubilization Root–Microbe Interactions Microbial Inoculants Sustainable Agriculture
FULL SUBJECT

Biofertilizers

Scientific Definition

Biofertilizers are biological preparations containing living microorganisms that, when applied to seed, soil or plant-associated environments, contribute to plant nutrition and growth by improving nutrient availability, nutrient cycling or beneficial biological interactions.

Scientific Overview

Biofertilizers examines the use of beneficial living microorganisms to improve plant nutrition and soil biological processes. The course introduces microbial groups used in biofertilization and explains mechanisms such as biological nitrogen fixation, phosphate solubilization, nutrient mobilization and beneficial interactions between microorganisms and plant roots. It also considers microbial inoculants as tools for sustainable crop production, soil fertility management and improved nutrient-use efficiency.

Core Areas

Beneficial microorganisms used as biofertilizers
Biological nitrogen fixation
Phosphate solubilization
Potassium mobilization
Plant growth-promoting microorganisms
Root–microbe interactions
Microbial inoculants
Soil microbial activity
Sustainable agriculture

Biotechnology Relevance

Biofertilizers connect microbial biotechnology with agricultural biotechnology through beneficial microbial inoculants that contribute to nutrient cycling, plant productivity, soil biological activity and sustainable crop production.

Key Scientific Principle

Biofertilizers act through biological processes and beneficial plant–microbe interactions rather than simply supplying nutrients in the same way as conventional synthetic fertilizers.

  5. Relate biofertilizers to sustainable agriculture and agricultural biotechnology.
